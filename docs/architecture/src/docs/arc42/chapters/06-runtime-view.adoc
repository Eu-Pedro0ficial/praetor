== Runtime View

Scenarios 1 through 4 describe the target governed pipeline; the milestone
subsections identify executable historical subsets. Scenarios 5 through 9 are
future unless their steps are explicitly attributed to an implemented
milestone. The traceability ledger remains authoritative for lifecycle and
ownership.

=== Scenario 1 — Attach to an Existing Legacy Project

[plantuml, "{plantUMLDir}dynamic-project-attach", png]
----
include::../diagrams/dynamic-project-attach.puml[]
----

. Engineer starts `praetor` in an existing repository; the interactive shell
  resolves and retains the active Project context and begins in root mode.
. Project Registry resolves the operational repository association and an existing memory repository or provisions one through the configured SCM adapter if authorized.
. Repository Intelligence scans source, manifests, history, tests and configuration.
. Memory Engine clones/updates the project memory repository and builds a local query projection.
. Memory Invalidation compares memory evidence fingerprints against current source state.
. Knowledge gaps are explicitly recorded rather than invented.
. A repository/context snapshot becomes available for future Change executions.

=== Scenario 2 — Plan a Change

. Intake normalizes issue/request intent.
. Workflow Engine creates a Change.
. Context Pack Builder retrieves relevant project and organization knowledge under a token/context budget.
. Repository Intelligence and Impact Engine identify likely affected symbols/modules and expected change surface.
. Specification Engine generates or validates a specification and its
  Specification Pack when the M1.3 workflow requires one.
. Planner role proposes a plan.
. Policy Engine evaluates whether the plan requires approval, violates constraints, or attempts protected operations.
. If required, an authorized human approves before implementation becomes available.

=== Scenario 3 — Implement and Verify a Change

[plantuml, "{plantUMLDir}dynamic-change-execution", png]
----
include::../diagrams/dynamic-change-execution.puml[]
----

. Workflow Engine opens an isolated Sandbox Runtime.
. Agent Router selects an allowed provider for the Implementer role.
. The implementer receives the approved spec, plan, bounded surface and context pack.
. Sandbox enforces the write boundary and returns a patch/diff.
. Verification discovery derives candidates from language/toolchain-independent
  repository evidence and, where ambiguous, a separate read-only
  `verification-planning` attempt.
. Praetor validates a structured VerificationPlan and the Verification Engine
  executes its admitted deterministic steps.
. Change Surface Control compares actual and authorized surface.
. Independent review roles evaluate the proposed change and evidence.
. Policy Engine determines pass, failure, exception-required, or human-approval-required state.
. Only after acceptance is the patch eligible to be applied/committed or submitted as a PR.

=== Scenario 4 — Unexpected Change Surface

A request in the `order` domain produces a modification in `authentication` outside the approved surface. The runtime:

. records the unexpected file/symbol and causal execution run;
. rejects or pauses the transition according to policy;
. does not let the implementer self-authorize the expansion;
. requires plan/surface revision and/or explicit human exception;
. preserves the violation as audit evidence.

==== M0.4 Executable Isolation and Patch Subset

The M0.4 executable subset implemented only the source/workspace and patch
portion of the broader implementation scenario:

. `change isolate` creates a process-local Change, advances it through
  `planned`, captures the M0.3 source boundary and creates a detached worktree
  at that exact clean revision;
. controlled proposal mutation targets only the worktree; there is no AI
  executor or generic command runner in M0.4;
. `change patch` extracts the complete Git patch and changed-path inventory;
. expected and possible paths produce a `surface-valid` retained proposal,
  while any protected or unexpected path rejects the whole proposal;
. surface-valid does not advance the Change beyond `isolated`; M0.6 owns
  deterministic evidence validation and M0.7 owns human approval;
. a rejected patch remains owned and inspectable until explicit retry or
  `change discard`; retry proves cleanup before creating a new workspace;
. session shutdown removes only the adapter-owned worktree, records
  append-oriented provenance, and leaves the Change recoverable without
  manufacturing rejection.

Dirty canonical source and externally detected source drift fail closed.
Praetor does not stash, reset, clean, repair or overwrite developer source.

The same M0.4 handlers are reachable by entering `change` and invoking
`isolate`, `patch`, or `discard` relative to that mode. `end` returns to root
without changing the retained Change. Contextual `?` resolves and displays
mode-valid command metadata without executing input or mutating session/domain
state.

==== M0.5 Executable Provider Subset

The M0.5 implementation extends the retained M0.4 proposal flow:

. the developer creates an active isolated proposal with `change isolate`;
. the composition root has exactly one compile-time registered adapter,
  `codex-cli`, and the session holds an explicit provider plus optional
  provider-scoped model selection;
. `change implement` creates a Praetor-owned execution-attempt identity and a
  provider-independent request linked to the Change, ApprovedScope, workspace,
  base revision, and source-state digest;
. the selected adapter serializes and accounts the exact provider request by
  fixed logical component before any provider lifecycle event or process
  invocation; an invalid or over-budget governance-critical request fails
  closed without invoking the provider;
. the canonical-source guard verifies the source boundary, then `codex-cli`
  performs bounded local version/capability preflight and runs non-interactive
  `codex exec` with the ProposalWorkspace as its process and Codex working
  root;
. the adapter returns bounded normalized provenance; its textual response and
  any claimed file list have no source authority;
. Git extracts the actual patch and complete changed-path set, and the M0.4
  service compares it to the ApprovedScope;
. an expected/possible patch is retained while the Change stays `isolated`;
  an empty, protected, unexpected, malformed, provider-failed, cancelled, or
  drifted attempt fails without deterministic-validation or approval claims.

Provider start/completion/failure and the resulting patch events share the
Praetor attempt identity. Every provider lifecycle event carries bounded
request-context accounting; completion also carries the provider's
authoritative input, cached-input, output, and reasoning token counts when
available. Bytes and characters are local serialization measurements and are
never presented as token estimates. Failed attempts record normalized failure
and Git-observed partial changed paths when available, never raw provider
output. The command cleans its process-owned failed proposal; canonical source
remains untouched and external drift is not repaired.

For implementation requests, intent is limited to 32 KiB. Explicit serialized
scope is limited to 4,096 paths and 256 KiB. The complete provider request is
limited to 512 KiB. Advisory impact context retains at most 64 entries and 16
KiB, with at most 1,000 bytes per projected line; omitted-entry counts and
truncation are explicit. Authorization and governance are never silently
truncated. Repository-wide scope sends the root authority marker and protected
paths, not the repository inventory or source bodies.

`provider list`, `provider show`, `provider select`, and `provider model`
expose only explicit session selection. There is no automatic provider
selection, fallback, capability/trust/data routing, ranking, load balancing,
or multi-provider execution in this scenario.

==== M0.6 Executable Verification Subset

The M0.6 implementation extends the retained M0.5 proposal flow with this
runtime sequence:

. consume the surface-valid Proposal, PatchArtifact, SourceSnapshot,
  Repository Context, ChangeIntent, and ApprovedScope;
. discover candidate checks from open-ended repository signals such as
  manifests, build/task/test/lint/typecheck configuration, CI declarations,
  toolchain files, and repository scripts;
. retain repository-declared, deterministically inferred, AI-assisted inferred,
  and user/configured origins without presenting AI inference as fact;
. when ambiguity remains, create a new `verification-planning` provider
  execution with a fresh context and `ExecutionAttemptId` distinct from the
  `implementation` attempt;
. enforce a read-only source/workspace boundary for that role and accept only
  structured candidate metadata, never source edits or arbitrary executable
  shell text;
. construct and validate a structured VerificationPlan, including executable,
  arguments, working-directory scope, provenance, timeout/cancellation,
  environment, and output constraints;
. execute the admitted build, test, lint, typecheck, patch-integrity, or other
  repository-supported steps through the deterministic Verification Engine;
. normalize actual tool results into an EvidenceSet linked to the source,
  patch, plan step, resolved tool identity, timestamps/duration, exit status,
  bounded/redacted output, outcome, and discovery provenance;
. permit only the existing `isolated -> validated` transition when all required
  deterministic evidence satisfies the minimum V0 gate;
. leave final human approval to M0.7.

Core V0 uses deterministic rules for `go.mod`, recognized `package.json`
scripts, and recognized Makefile targets while treating other bounded
manifests, CI, and tool configuration as potential planning evidence. The
engine executes admitted executable-plus-argument-vector templates directly,
never through `sh -c`, `bash -c`, `eval`, or interpolation. It resolves every
step before starting any child process, confines working directories to the
ProposalWorkspace, uses a minimal credential-free environment, and captures
bounded output. All admitted steps are required. It continues sequentially to
collect evidence after an ordinary failed step, except when caller
cancellation prevents later execution, and always adds a patch-integrity
result. Empty or otherwise non-viable plans fail closed.

Planning and verification lifecycle events are append-oriented and separately
identify the provider `ExecutionAttemptId` and random
`VerificationAttemptId`. A planning failure may be recorded and deterministic
verification may continue only when repository evidence already yields a
viable plan; insufficient knowledge remains unvalidated. A failed gate leaves
the Change `isolated` and the retained proposal available for inspection or
discard.

The planning provider may be `codex-cli` with a separately captured model or
provider default, but M0.6 neither routes automatically nor requires a second
provider. Verification planning is not the mature M1.4 semantic Reviewer and
cannot declare the patch correct.

==== M0.7 Explicit Human Disposition Subset

The M0.7 runtime sequence is:

. receive an explicit local interactive `approve` or `reject` command;
. show a bounded Change, patch, changed-path, and verification/evidence
  summary;
. require `validated` and verify exact Project/Change/workspace,
  PatchArtifact, source digest, patch digest, VerificationAttempt, and passing
  EvidenceSet linkage;
. reuse the retained proposal and canonical-source integrity guard;
. append `HUMAN_DECISION_RECORDED` with bounded rationale and
  `local-interactive-human` provenance;
. recheck integrity and request the existing audited transition to `approved`
  or `rejected`.

The decision event is intentionally before the transition event. A failed
decision append leaves state `validated`; a later transition failure may leave
the attempted decision in truthful append-oriented history while state stays
unchanged. No transactionality or authenticated actor identity is claimed.
Neither disposition is automatically audit-locked, and no patch is applied to
canonical source by M0.7. The process-local decision/proposal context remains
available for the separate M0.8 operation.

==== M0.8 Canonical Integration and Closure

The M0.8 approved sequence is:

. receive explicit `change apply` or contextual `apply` for an `approved`
  Change;
. validate exact Change, Project, ProposalWorkspace, PatchArtifact, source,
  VerificationAttempt, passing EvidenceSet, and `APPROVE` linkage;
. verify proposal/canonical integrity and run `git apply --check`;
. append `CANONICAL_APPLICATION_STARTED`, then repeat integrity and preflight
  under the adapter lock before mutation;
. apply the retained patch bytes through stdin to the canonical working tree;
. prove exact canonical diff, path inventory and patch digest while HEAD and
  the Git index remain unchanged;
. inspect the intentionally dirty resulting SourceSnapshot and append
  `CANONICAL_APPLICATION_COMPLETED`;
. transition `approved -> audit-locked`, then remove the owned proposal
  workspace.

The rejected sequence uses explicit `change close` or contextual `close`.
It validates the exact `REJECT` linkage, proves canonical source remains the
approved clean base, appends `CHANGE_CLOSURE_RECORDED`, rechecks integrity,
transitions `rejected -> audit-locked`, and removes the workspace. It never
invokes canonical application.

Default `git apply` whole-patch behavior is used without `--reject`, `--3way`,
`--index`, `--cached`, or `--unsafe-paths`. No commit, push, merge, branch, or
PR is created. If mutation succeeds but later proof or audit fails, the Change
is not falsely audit-locked, the error/result reports mutation, and no rollback
is claimed. A repeat attempt fails closed because canonical source no longer
matches the approved base. Empty input, help, completion, provider completion,
verification, approval alone, and session close never apply source.

==== M0.9 Interactive Presentation

. At composition, Praetor loads versioned presentation preferences from the
  user configuration directory, or uses safe defaults when no file exists.
. Before each interactive read, the shell obtains current terminal dimensions,
  preferences, and the shared session status snapshot.
. The renderer emits a sanitized Engineering Console. It renders the configured
  sidebar only when enabled and sufficient command width remains; temporary
  narrow-terminal suppression never changes the stored preference.
. `configure layout` commands validate and atomically persist a complete
  candidate before replacing process-local preferences.
. Readline retains ownership of editing, history, completion, cancellation,
  prompt redisplay, and the input cursor. Rendering cannot execute commands or
  alter Project, Change, provider, verification, decision, audit, or source
  state.
. In full-screen terminals, logical history is sanitized and reflowed to the
  current command-surface width before vertical viewport selection. Resize
  recomputes display rows; non-TTY output is dispatched without this wrapping.
. A binary compiled with the explicit `praetor_debug` build tag may attach one
  presentation recorder before the shell starts. It creates a private XDG STATE
  transcript, records bounded/redacted commands and logical Praetor output in
  order, records the session outcome, and fails explicitly on create/write
  errors. Ordinary builds have no operational `--debug` path. Provider raw
  streams and terminal control output do not enter this recorder.

==== M1.0 Local Policy Evaluation

. After successful deterministic verification and the existing transition to
  `validated`, load the fixed Project Policy Manifest V1.
. Validate its bounded YAML schema and compute the exact manifest digest.
. Evaluate each generic rule against the retained EvidenceSet, preserving
  individual outcomes and accumulating denial, review, and approval
  requirements.
. Append `POLICY_DECISION_RECORDED` before retaining the decision.
. Require the exact policy/evidence authority chain for positive human
  disposition and canonical application. `AUTO` retains Core V0 human
  acceptance; `REVIEW` and `FORBIDDEN` block positive disposition; rejection
  remains available.
. An optional `POLICY_EXCEPTION_CANDIDATE_RECORDED` never changes the original
  outcome and grants no exception.

Policy evaluation performs no Change transition and no source mutation.

==== M1.1 Durable Authority and Recovery

. Project attach validates the per-Project SQLite store's metadata,
  compatibility, required pragmas, structural authority, and recovery
  conditions without eagerly hashing every historical artifact, repairing
  authority, or implicitly migrating legacy audit.
. Change creation binds `(ProjectId, ChangeId)` to the exact embedded Core V0
  WorkflowSnapshot bytes, identity/version/schema, and digest.
. Each authoritative transition supplies ExpectedRevision and atomically
  commits the new Change revision, immutable artifacts/relations, versioned
  bindings, operation result, and required audit events, or commits none.
. Read-only inspection reconstructs exact Change/workflow/artifact/audit
  authority and reports recovery blockers without migration or repair.
. Selecting an in-flight Change in a fresh process validates bound artifact
  kind, ownership, digest, schema, and dependency linkage; it reconstructs the
  proposal/evidence/policy/human-decision projection and reattaches only the
  exact retained Git worktree under the controlled temporary-workspace layout.
. A consequential external operation commits its durable reservation before
  the effect. Canonical Git mutation then takes a short Project lock under XDG
  STATE, revalidates PRE, applies once, validates POST, and records completion
  while exclusion remains held.
. After interruption, explicit recovery classifies PRE, POST, AMBIGUOUS,
  divergence, corruption, or incompatibility. It never automatically repeats
  an unknown external effect or invents a successful Change transition. For an
  already completed operation, its immutable structured Result must match the
  independently proven exact POST before terminal Change/ApplicationResult
  authority can be published.

These semantics are implemented under ADR-033 through ADR-038. Later workflow,
review, repository-intelligence, and memory capabilities remain outside M1.1.

==== M1.2 Implemented Repository and Impact Analysis

[plantuml, "{plantUMLDir}dynamic-impact-analysis", png]
----
include::../diagrams/dynamic-impact-analysis.puml[]
----

. Inspect the repository before analysis and derive the exact composite source
  fingerprint, including sorted untracked status-name evidence but excluding
  untracked bytes from analysis.
. Reuse file-local cached results only when content, analyzer, schema, and
  configuration keys match; otherwise rebuild them. Recompute global graph
  relationships and KnowledgeGaps.
. Inspect source again. Discard the candidate when the comparable fingerprint
  changed during analysis; publish only a coherent immutable model.
. Traverse only approved relation types from the Change intent/start scope with
  visited-set and depth/cardinality limits. Record explanation paths and gaps.
. Derive expected, possible, protected, and uncertain impact plus the advisory
  RiskProfile. `INDETERMINATE` records insufficient evidence outside the
  ordered low/moderate/high severity scale.
. Atomically append an immutable Change-owned ImpactReport through the existing
  artifact authority and bind its exact ArtifactId. Do not expand ApprovedScope.
. For current use, prove that the report's model build key is still current;
  stale or unknown inputs require rebuild/new analysis rather than silent use.

This sequence is implemented under ADR-039 and ADR-040. Analyzer failure,
panic, unsafe path input, unsupported syntax,
excluded untracked content, traversal truncation, and cache loss remain
explicit rather than being converted to absence of impact.

==== M1.3 Target Specification Pack Authority

. Normalize a requirement into a candidate Specification Pack.
. Validate completeness, provenance, identity, version, and governing source
  links using the approved future schema.
. Require any configured human Specification/Plan approval before
  implementation.
. Bind proposal, evidence, policy, review, and disposition to the exact
  approved digests.
. Invalidate downstream authority when a requirement, Specification, or Plan
  changes.

The candidate `engineering/specs/` location and this authority chain remain
future and decision-gated; no current runtime behavior implements them.

==== Cross-Cutting Target Inspection, Presentation, and Operability

. M1.1 exposes durable artifact inspection and recovers safely across local
  concurrency and interruption under ADR-033 through ADR-037.
. M1.3 projects source-linked Specification/Change traceability while retaining
  freshness and knowledge gaps.
. M1.4 renders the exact review state, blocker, consequence, rework, and
  available human action without creating a new authority.
. M1.5 bounds cancellation, resources, credentials, network/process exposure,
  and diagnostics for quality/security tool execution.

These are acceptance concerns of approved milestones, not separate roadmap
milestones. Their representation and operating boundaries remain subject to
the owning milestone gates.

. A run, PR review or postmortem generates an observation.

=== Scenario 6 — Memory Invalidation After Source Change

. A source commit modifies a file/symbol referenced by active memory.
. Fingerprint comparison identifies potentially stale records.
. Memory state becomes `NEEDS_REVALIDATION`/`STALE` according to policy; the old record is not silently rewritten.
. Future Context Packs avoid representing stale knowledge as active fact.
. Revalidation may create a successor record that supersedes the previous one.

=== Scenario 7 — Rejected PR Becomes Learning Signal

A human rejects a PR because the change violates a local convention not yet formalized. The system records the review reason, links it to the Change, proposes a Convention or Policy Candidate, and requires normal promotion governance. Rejection therefore improves future context without silently training or mutating an AI model.

=== Scenario 8 — Project Knowledge Promoted to Organization Knowledge

Repeated, validated project-level knowledge may become a candidate for Organization Memory. Promotion is governed separately and may require a higher authority. Organization policies can flow downward to projects, with explicit project exceptions where allowed.

=== Scenario 9 — M1.5 Composed Quality and Security Gate

. The runtime identifies applicable current-run quality/security capabilities and
  prefers an approved existing integration when one is available.
. Otherwise, mature policy may select an approved local/open-source capability
  fallback whose lower or different assurance is retained explicitly. This
  future tool fallback does not introduce M0.6 AI-provider fallback.
. DAST runs only when an authorized runnable target, environment, network,
  dependencies, and test data make it applicable; otherwise availability or
  non-applicability is recorded rather than reported as pass.
. Deterministic tool results, external quality/security evidence, and AI
  semantic findings are normalized without collapsing their authority.
. The mature Policy Engine composes the evidence into a Quality Gate outcome
  such as `PASS`, `REVIEW`, or `FAIL`; AI findings cannot override a
  deterministic failure by authority.
. Project or Organization Memory may increase scrutiny or suggest additional
  checks, but only executed checks become deterministic evidence.
