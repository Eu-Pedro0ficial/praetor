# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

Praetor is an engineering project built around explicit governance, evidence, and human responsibility.

We want its community to reflect the same values.

We pledge to make participation in the project a respectful and harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity or expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that contributes to a healthy project include:

- being respectful, specific, and constructive in technical disagreement;
- criticizing ideas, code, evidence, or decisions rather than people;
- explaining trade-offs and assumptions instead of asserting authority;
- accepting that good engineering decisions may change when new evidence appears;
- respecting documented project boundaries and contribution processes;
- giving and receiving feedback professionally;
- acknowledging mistakes and correcting them transparently;
- helping maintain a collaborative environment for contributors with different levels of experience;
- respecting privacy, security reports, and confidential disclosure channels.

Strong technical disagreement is welcome. Personal hostility is not.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes, but is not limited to:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or targeted hostility;
- discriminatory or derogatory comments;
- personal insults or sustained bad-faith interaction;
- sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted sexual attention;
- deliberate disruption of issues, pull requests, reviews, or project infrastructure;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- retaliating against someone for raising a conduct or security concern;
- knowingly misrepresenting evidence, authorship, test results, or project decisions;
- using project spaces primarily for spam, self-promotion, or unrelated solicitation;
- other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional engineering environment.

## Technical discussions

Praetor explicitly values evidence over confidence.

Accordingly, contributors are encouraged to challenge proposals, architecture, implementation choices, and assumptions. Technical criticism should remain focused on the work and should be supported by reasoning, constraints, reproducible behavior, tests, measurements, or other relevant evidence whenever practical.

No contributor, maintainer, tool, or AI system is exempt from review because of status or confidence.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to all official project spaces, including:

- issues;
- pull requests and reviews;
- discussions;
- project documentation and contribution channels;
- other spaces where someone is officially representing Praetor.

## Reporting conduct concerns

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, do not escalate the situation publicly when a private report is more appropriate.

Contact the project maintainer privately using the contact information available through the maintainer's GitHub profile.

Reports will be reviewed in context and handled as confidentially as reasonably possible. Information may be shared when necessary to investigate, protect participants, or comply with applicable obligations.

Bad-faith reports or deliberate abuse of the reporting process may themselves be treated as a conduct violation.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing standards of acceptable behavior and may take proportionate corrective action, including:

1. **Correction** — a private or public request to change inappropriate behavior.
2. **Warning** — a formal warning with expectations for future participation.
3. **Temporary restriction** — temporary limits on interaction with project spaces.
4. **Permanent restriction** — removal from project participation for sustained or severe violations.

The response will consider severity, context, impact, prior behavior, and whether the participant acts constructively after receiving feedback.
